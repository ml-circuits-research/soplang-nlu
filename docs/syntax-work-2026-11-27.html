Decisions and insights of the syntax work, 2026-11-27

Written 2026-11-27; revised the same day after two reviews.

Related documents: The VSS reader: architecture (how VSS reads a message, step by step, and every rule it holds) and How syntactic labels are made and measured (levels, judges, benchmark, agreement, conversion of the dataset).

On 2026-11-27 the formalizers of soplang-nlu (programs that turn one message into a circuit of SOP Lang) moved from the detailed nlu schema to a small, purely syntactic schema. Every strategy was rewritten to produce it straight from the text, the WebMeet dataset was split into a semantic and a syntactic layer, and a third of its messages received a syntax circuit. This document records what was decided that day and why, what was measured, what surprised, what was tried and dropped, and what is still open. It is written for a reviewer who has to judge the decisions and for whoever continues the work. It does not repeat the schema specification (docs/syntax-schema.md) and does not describe the VSS reader in detail (docs/strategies/vss.md, docs/vss-architecture.html); the evaluation and conversion method has its own document (docs/syntax-evaluation-and-conversion.html). Every number says what it counts and where it was measured. Numbers recounted for this document from files are marked "recounted for this document". Numbers that the reviewers of this document computed from the git history, and that this revision did not check again, are marked "checked during review". The run folders changed after the commits described here, so a number is cited at the commit named with it.

2. The goal and how it is measured

Every strategy writes the syntax schema from the text alone, and as many of its circuits as possible are acceptable: ideally over 99% of messages (improvements-ideas.md, "Goal").

The measure is judged acceptance. For one strategy and one sample, GOAL = (messages at L8 + L9 + L10) / (all messages of the sample). A message whose circuit has not been judged yet stands in the column "waiting". It counts in the denominator but not as accepted, so while circuits wait GOAL is a lower bound, and judging them can only raise it (tableOf in tests/benchmark/syntax/lib.mjs, used by table.mjs).

The owner refused to restate the goal as "precision on the readings a strategy does not flag, plus coverage". The stated reason: the dataset itself is to be corrected through a judged loop until the comparison with it is exact. Every acceptance therefore also writes a label (decision D6), and a later run that writes the same circuit gets L9 without a judge.

Not obvious from the code: what limits a measured 99%

In the morning, in the nlu schema, VSS readings identical to a circuit the dataset already held as accepted were accepted by the judges only 93% of the time; docs/strategies/vss.md, "Where the design stands", concludes "so no design can measure near 99%". The judges of that loop thus rejected about 7 in 100 circuits that had been accepted before. Agreement between two judges on the same circuits in the syntax schema was not measured on 2026-11-27, so whether the simpler schema and rules reduce this disagreement is not known.

The judging rule

Judges grade by one question, the criterion of the external reviewer that the owner adopted (tests/benchmark/syntax/rules.md):

Can the next layer continue from this circuit by completing it, or must it first undo a wrong statement?

A circuit fails only when it states something wrong or loses information the words give. A rejection must quote the words and name the wrong statement or the lost information; "if you cannot quote the words, the circuit is acceptable". Plausible and incomplete readings pass. Several readings pass where grammar allows several: the attachment site of a relation with a marker (the clause, or any nominal of that clause that grammar allows, with or without attachment=ambiguous), which earlier wire a coordination joins, a word that can be a noun or a verb, a misspelt word left where it stands. Not judged: what a complement is to the verb, what a modifier says, which clause has a discourse word, the order and names of wires, whether a question is meant as a request, what a pronoun refers to.

Example of a rejection from the random benchmark (eval-results/2026-11-27-syntax-benchmark-random/judging/, circuit written by VSS):

# node Intro to 260, 240
@n1 syntax.nominal head="node" dependent="Intro"
@r1 syntax.relation marker="to" from=$n1 to=$n2
@n2 syntax.nominal head="260"
@n3 syntax.nominal head="240"
@c1 syntax.clause form=fragment complement=$k1
@k1 syntax.coordination conjunct=$n1 conjunct=$n3

judge: L3  In 'to 260, 240' the number '240' is coordinated with 'node' instead of paired with '260'.

Where the rules came from

The criterion and the first settled cases came from the judging rules of the nlu schema, strategies/vss/judging-rules.md. The criterion first appeared there in 8fb0205 (11:31, "plausible judging rules"). 615e232 added settled cases on attaching a phrase to the nearest noun when grammar allows it, comma lists, the choice of head, and underspecification marks in lists; 9196fe7 let pass any coordination scope that grammar allows. The rules of the syntax schema, tests/benchmark/syntax/rules.md, were created in e166870 (13:26) and grew each time judges ruled differently on the same kind of case or asked: 24de853 added five settled cases (a conjunction between two modifiers, quantity phrases such as "a pair of trees", two bare nouns after a marker, fixed phrases such as "from now on", a participle that names), and 01695d3 six from round 1 (a command needs its verb; degree modifiers such as "a bit longer"; mentioned words, as in "a paragraph saying Thank you"; comma-separated times; "left of"; a question-word clause after a verb of telling must be attached). The attribution of these commits was checked during review. Later cases ("let's", "everything else", embedded question clauses are declarative) came from a81dd93 (improvements-ideas.md).

Why it is done this way: L9 needs no judge, and labels are frozen per run

A label is a circuit some judge accepted, or that strategies of different families wrote alike (T1). A circuit equal to it is accepted already, so L9 costs nothing. But labels change: a benchmark publication replaces a label with the circuit a judge accepted in that run (judge-publish.mjs). If a table were recomputed against today's labels, a strategy whose circuit equalled yesterday's label would lose its L9 for no fault of its own. Each run therefore keeps the labels its messages had when judging was first prepared, in key/labels-before.jsonl, written once (tests/benchmark/syntax/README.md, "The table"). The session notes record it as the answer to the problem described in I6.

The README describes the newer format of that file: one line per message of the run that had a label, with the keys text, label, source, accepted_by. The file of the random benchmark was written by the earlier tools: it holds every label of the dataset at that moment (60,914 lines, keys text, label, core, run, source, previous). readLabelsBefore reads both formats (comment above labelsBeforeFile in tests/benchmark/syntax/lib.mjs), and levelOf looks up only the run's messages, so the table counts 593 labelled messages over the run's texts.

3. The schema change and why

Until the morning of 2026-11-27 the formalizers wrote the nlu schema (schemas/nlu/schema.sop), which has more than 50 keys and needed the knowledge repository (sop-knowledge-worlds) to check circuits. It separated, among others, direct object, indirect object, predicative entity, topic and participant; adverb, particle, result complement and degree; several kinds of relation. The day replaced it with the syntax schema, version 3: four wire types and 18 keys.

A real message and the circuit three strategies (prolog, spacy, z3) wrote alike for it, accepted by a judge in the random benchmark (packet line 1):

# make a bar chart of the projects by name please
@c1 syntax.clause form=imperative verb="make" discourse="please" complement=$n1
@n1 syntax.nominal dependent="a" dependent="bar" head="chart"
@r1 syntax.relation marker="of" from=$n1 to=$n2

For the same message VSS and DistilBERT write the relation of "by name" with "projects" as its attachment site and no ambiguity mark: @r2 syntax.relation marker="by" from=$n2 to=$n3, with the other five wires the same (first-run/vss.jsonl and first-run/distilbert.jsonl in the benchmark folder, the files judged at 9f57aa3). The two circuits differ only in the attachment site of a relation with a marker, so under relaxed agreement (T2) they count as written alike.

Rule 1: every word exactly once

The schema has no positions. Every word of the message is covered by exactly one quoted value, and a value holds words, not positions. When the same words occur more than once, values claim occurrences in the order the circuit is written, top to bottom and left to right, and each value takes the earliest occurrence whose words are still free (docs/syntax-schema.md, Rule 1). In the specification's example, the order of the wires says which "box" is the complement of "put":

# put the box on the box
@c1 syntax.clause form=imperative verb="put" complement=$n1
@n1 syntax.nominal dependent="the" head="box"
@r1 syntax.relation from=$c1 to=$n2 marker="on" attachment=ambiguous
@n2 syntax.nominal dependent="the" head="box"

@n1 is written first, so it claims the first "the" and the first "box". A formalizer does not work this order out by hand: writeCircuit in lib/syntax.mjs takes the wires with the position of each value and writes them in an order that satisfies the rule.

Not obvious from the code: punctuation and repeated words

Punctuation stands in no wire. Version 2 asked every mark to be covered. With marks covered, a clause would claim commas far apart while other wires claim the commas between them, and under Rule 1 no order of wires could align such crossing claims. Which clause has a comma was already "not compared", so the marks carried no structure, only this cost (specification, decision 7).

Crossing repeated words are written in the order of the message. When one coordination holds the first and third "and" of a message and another the second, no order of the two wires gives each value its own occurrence, because a value always takes the earliest free one. Since commit d12c190, writeCircuit then writes the wires in the order of the message instead of refusing the circuit: two values that hold the same word are the same in a circuit, and every word is still covered once. Measured: the case occurs in 2 to 8 of 1,831 messages, depending on the strategy, mostly with "and", "then" and "right" (specification, Rule 1, known limit). No real example is shown here: once written, the circuit cannot show which occurrence a strategy meant, since only its internal positions record that, and none was extracted for this document.

Why the change

  • The layer states what the words show, and nothing that needs meaning. The specification's purpose: split the message into clauses and nominals, give every word one place, relate the wires; who receives, what is the result, what a pronoun refers to and whether a question is a request are for the next layer (docs/syntax-schema.md, "Purpose"). The nlu distinctions listed above each map to one key of syntax (the table "How it relates to the nlu schema" in the specification).
  • No dependence on another repository. The schema, its checker and its writer are in soplang-nlu itself: schemas/syntax/schema.sop, lib/syntax.mjs (wordsOf, writeCircuit, validateSyntax, coreOf), tests/syntax-contract.test.mjs. Before, the common reader skipped the check when no knowledge repository was configured, "so a returned result proved nothing" (specification, decision 8).
  • Established names. Version 1 (commit 7a90ade, 16 keys) called a clause a fact, a nominal a thing, and had keys such as doer, action, how. A first review asked for names that do not ask for a meaning (doer became subject: in "the box was moved by Ana" the subject is the box). An intermediate draft used everyday words (main, via, other, start=open); the owner asked for the established terms of grammar and dependency syntax instead, since such terms exist and everyday words read as metaphors. Version 3 (commit 8536c9c) uses them; each name and its source is in the specification, decision 6.
  • The reviewer's cases are written with the four types. A second review asked that "make A blue and B green" show the shared verb (key ellipsis on the fragment) and that "near Ben but not behind Jo" show that "but" joins two relations and "not" negates only the second (relations as conjuncts, negation on a relation). Both were adopted (specification, decision 8).

What the smaller schema changes by itself

Measured before the change, with VSS readings of the fixed sample written in nlu and compared by identity with circuits already accepted in the dataset (docs/syntax-schema.md, "What was measured"): 61.8% identical in the full nlu schema, 62.2% in version 1 of the syntax schema, 62.4% when the head is not compared, 64.8% when the clause is also allowed as the attachment site of a relation. The schema alone explains 0.4 to 3 points; most rejections are about what the syntax schema still asks for (which word is the verb, which words form one nominal, where a phrase attaches). Making the head required changes the identity comparison for 4 of 1,831 messages; its effect on judged acceptance was not measured.

Reviewer suggestions not adopted

The specification records each one with its reason (decisions 2 to 6). In short: a key giving each value's position in the message (rejected: numbers on every wire that a person cannot check by eye); two circuits for an ambiguous attachment instead of one with attachment=ambiguous (rejected: three ambiguous phrases make eight circuits); a relation that starts at another relation, for "two inches from the edge" (rejected: a second level of structure for every tool; the measure is a nominal and the relation starts at it); "make" as a clause of its own completed by two verbless clauses (rejected: the first conjunct would look different from "make A blue" alone); the names object for complement and noun_phrase for nominal.

I4. Retraining VSS after a change of the legend or of the word-form rules shifts unrelated readings

The route book is re-estimated from scratch by expectation-maximization at every training (strategies/vss/train.mjs, src/routes.mjs), so a change of the legend or of src/forms.mjs moves probabilities everywhere. The practice adopted: add marks as new open lists (open=true: they add a place and confine nothing, see the legend's header), retrain once, and check the damage right away on accepted circuits. A change of the stem rule so that "goes" is a form of "go" was dropped for this reason (the shifted readings were seen; no count was recorded).

Not obvious from the code: why "goes" is not related to "go"

stemsOf in strategies/vss/src/forms.mjs keeps a stem only when at least three letters remain ("using" is a form of "use", not of "us"). Stripping "-es" from "goes" leaves "go", two letters, so the stem is never proposed. The expectation was that lowering the limit would relate many short words and that retraining would then move readings far from "go"; this was not measured beyond the shifted readings seen.

A later change in the working tree, not part of the commits described here, takes another way: kindsOf in strategies/vss/src/reader.mjs lets a verb of two letters take the endings when its verb counts are looked up ("goes", "going"), without changing src/forms.mjs and without retraining.
