# "Meaning as a Path" (the book of the original VSS): digest for the strict-vss design

Source: https://scriptahub.com/books/meaning/as/a/path/bk-85adcf86fc264f11/en/full_content.html (read in full by an agent; quotes are short).

## Thesis
- "A sentence becomes a chart of answers to fixed questions." Meaning in a world = the answers to that world's questions (the legend), including "it does not say".
- "Two sentences mean the same thing exactly when they give the same answers to the same questions." Meaning is relative to a domain.
- Ch. 11: "The legend is the system's schema." A legend can be proposed, refined, approved and "declared, not learned".

## Terms (book → technical)
- sky: the whole space of phase vectors (D≈1024); dial: one angle.
- star: a written point. Word star f[w]: fixed random identity from the letters; named star R[r]: a role vector of the legend; chart star: a word star shifted into the sentence.
- shift / jump / key: binding (phase addition, ⊗); inverse key: unbinding (conjugate). Keys learned per category K[c]; relative keys per pair of categories.
- probe: the moving part; position a = a ⊗ K[category]; pilot: the reader (encoder), knows no facts.
- route book: the only learned store (keys per category, relative keys per pair, one gate per category).
- chart / constellation: the tally of stars drawn (bundling, +); the state S; a "hologram": stars can be tested, not listed.
- capacity: "a few dozen terms sit cleanly in a constellation" at ~1000 dials; hundreds blur. "The honest range of a chart is a single event." Long texts are "structures of states" (ch. 12): several charts linked by keys.
- legend: the world's questions, each with a named star; domain: {R[r] ⊗ f[w]} the region of a role.
- frame: the answers read through the legend (one per role, with margin and occupancy marker m).
- scanner / cleanup: nearest-star search after unbinding; margin: best minus second ("where 'I'm not sure' lives").
- remainder / residual: what the frame does not explain; its direction reveals a missing legend question.
- level / reported: R[reported] shifts the whole legend to another origin, so an inner sentence keeps its own roles; nesting = repeated application; trained on two levels, read three ("the pilot composes what it has keys for").
- beacon: a mark placed by a word ("a noun will be at my position + the relative key"), taken by a later word, erased at sentence end.
- gate / opener: a learned number per category saying whether its words open frames; verbs, names, nouns come out closed; "to", "from", "that" open.
- held aside: a word with no beacon yet is kept off the chart and placed when a beacon for its kind appears (backward keys).
- three drawings: each word placed at the probe's full position, at a pair-of-kinds key, and from the last opener; the copies vote.
- atlas: store of recent constellations (used for pronouns).
- world's constellation S*: the correct chart built from the answers; training maximises cos(S, S*); equivalence "is induced by the shared targets", not labelled on pairs.

## Limits named by the book
- binding commutes: paths form "a grid, not a tree" (order of operators lost);
- structure announced afterwards ("Paul gives a book to Anna, says Maria") is a limit of forward reading;
- a chart holds a single event; long texts need structures of states;
- categories are not discovered (dictionary or tagger); unknown category = broken key;
- results: constructed worlds, unseen depth read at 0.97–1.0; WikiSQL selected column 0.82; SNIPS slot F1 0.39 (kinds from skip-gram clusters put unseen names in unrelated clusters).
