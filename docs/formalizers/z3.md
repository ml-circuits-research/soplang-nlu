# Z3 (experimental)

This page: a description of the experimental Z3 formalizer, which writes English grammar as constraints and solves one optimisation problem per message. Kind: Explanation.

## What it is

A grammar of English written by hand as logical constraints, solved by the [Z3 solver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z3_Theorem_Prover). Z3 is a solver for [satisfiability modulo theories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satisfiability_modulo_theories) (SMT): given logical statements, it finds values that make them true.

Here each message is one **MaxSMT** problem. Every hard constraint must hold, and among the solutions the solver picks one where the soft constraints that hold weigh the most. This is the SMT form of [maximum satisfiability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_satisfiability_problem).

The code is in `strategies/z3/src/syntax/`; the model of constraints is `model.mjs`. The solver is the `z3-solver` package (version 5.4.1), Z3 compiled to [WebAssembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WebAssembly), so it runs inside Node.js.

## How it reads a message

1. **The lexicon** (`lexicon.mjs`) proposes what each word may be. Function words and common verbs come from `english.mjs`. Nouns, verbs and describing words come from a usage table, `strategies/z3/model/usage.json`. Endings and position supply the rest.
2. **The model** (`model.mjs`) states the choices. For every word, Z3 chooses one class the lexicon allows, and whether it is the head or a dependent of a nominal. For each verb, it chooses the construction that licenses it: a command, a finite verb after its subject, a verb after an auxiliary or after "to", a participle, and so on. For each phrase with a marker (a linking word such as "on" or "of"), it chooses where the phrase attaches: at the clause, at the nominal before it, or left open.
3. **Hard constraints** state what English grammar excludes. **Weighted soft constraints** state what it prefers. Fixed tie-breakers make a message always get the same circuit.
4. **The solver** (`solver.mjs`) solves the problem, one message at a time.
5. **The assembler** (`assemble.mjs`) lays the solution out as wires, and the circuit is written.

The usage table is learned from message text alone by `tools/train-usage.mjs`. It counts word order around function words. A word after a determiner and before a preposition heads a nominal. A word that opens a clause before a determiner, or follows "to" or a modal, is a verb. A word after "very", or closing a clause after "is" or "it", describes.

## Speed and cost

Tens of milliseconds per message, with a long tail of slow messages; Node.js only, plus the large WebAssembly file of the solver. Comparison: [Speed and cost](speed.md). Numbers: [STATUS](../STATUS.md#speed-and-cost-per-strategy).

## Known limits

- **Many open attachments.** A phrase attaches at the nominal before it only where the words settle it ("of", a measure, a clause with no verb and one nominal). Otherwise it starts at the clause when no nominal stands between, and is left open (`attachment=ambiguous`) when one does. So many accepted circuits leave the attachment open; the share is in [STATUS](../STATUS.md#the-benchmark-live).
- **The slow tail.** Most messages are solved in tens of milliseconds, but the slowest take far longer. Messages that slow would be felt while someone types ([Speed and cost](speed.md)).

## Why experimental

Z3 is still measured, kept runnable and a voice in agreement for its own family, but not improved for now: the owner's decision (called parked until 2026-11-27). Prolog and Z3 both stand for the fourth place of one hand-written grammar; which one keeps it is open ([decision D10](../project/decisions.md#d10)). An idea recorded in the journal as the owner's remark, not tried: carry the rules of hmm-rules into Z3 later, if a fourth independent voice is needed ([improvements-ideas.md](../../improvements-ideas.md), "Ideas not yet tried").
