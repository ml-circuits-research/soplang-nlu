1. What VSS is for, and its constraints

VSS is one of the formalization strategies of soplang-nlu. A formalization strategy reads one message and writes a circuit: lines of SOP Lang (the project's declarative notation, one statement per line) that split the message into clauses and nominals, give every word one place, and tie the parts together. Each line is a wire (the SOP Lang term): an identifier such as @c1, a type such as syntax.clause, then key=value pairs, where a value is a quoted run of the message's words or a reference $id to another wire. The messages come from the WebMeet dataset (webmeet-dataset): commands and questions typed to an application that draws pictures, diagrams, charts, documents and calendar entries. The target is the syntax schema, version 3, defined in docs/syntax-schema.md and checked by lib/syntax.mjs.

Plain words for the four wire types of the schema: a clause is one verb with what belongs to it (one command, statement or question), or a group of words with no verb (a fragment); a nominal is a noun with the words that depend on it, or a word that stands where a noun could (a pronoun, a name, a number); a relation ties two wires, usually through a linking word such as "on" or "if"; a coordination puts wires side by side ("A and B"). Inside a nominal the head is the main word and the others are its dependents.

The message "Move the blue box towards the yellow circle." becomes, through the common reader (lib/reader.mjs, the code every strategy is called through; three of these four lines are asserted by tests/vss-syntax.test.mjs, and the fourth, @n2, was checked for this document):

@c1 syntax.clause form=imperative verb="Move" complement=$n1
@n1 syntax.nominal dependent="the" dependent="blue" head="box"
@r1 syntax.relation marker="towards" from=$c1 to=$n2
@n2 syntax.nominal dependent="the" dependent="yellow" head="circle"

Why it is done this way

Why the project keeps a reader that scores below spaCy. VSS is a second voice whose source is unlike a parser's. The dataset is labelled largely by agreement between strategies that read a message from different sources: improvements-ideas.md records "62,066 of 183,497 messages have a syntactic circuit (33.8%): 56,934 by agreement of VSS and spaCy" (more exactly: 56,934 labels were settled by agreement, and 55,648 of them by VSS and spacy-direct alone; the rest come from six calendar files where an older DistilBERT took part, 106 of them without VSS, and from round 2; syntax-work-2026-11-27.html, "The dataset"), and "Families of strategies that read the same parse agree with each other on 77–100% of messages and repeat the same faults: agreement needs different sources". The same file states the owner's decision to keep "four strategies at most … they read a message from really different sources, so their agreement means something". A reader whose stations are learned from raw text and whose rules name only function words is such a different source.

Why it is not trained on the accepted circuits. 55,648 of the 62,066 labels were written by VSS agreeing with spacy-direct (and 880 more by VSS agreeing with an older DistilBERT, with or without spacy-direct), so training VSS on them would partly train it on its own output, and its agreement with spaCy would then mean less. The lever study of 2026-11-27 also measured that a station prior taken from the accepted circuits would gain about 1.4 points (section 14).

Why no dependencies and no annotated input. docs/strategies/vss.md states the constraints ("It needs no parser, no annotated circuits and no knowledge world"; "the same code runs in Node.js and in a browser") but the repository does not record why they were chosen: not recorded.

The repository does not expand the name "VSS". The vector operations in strategies/vss/src/space.mjs are those of vector symbolic architectures (also called hyperdimensional computing): every symbol gets a fixed random vector of +1 and −1 derived from its name, binding multiplies two vectors element by element (the result is unlike both inputs, and it is used to tag a filler with its role or a neighbour with its side), bundling adds vectors into one, and the cosine measures how alike two vectors are. VSS uses them for word vectors, for occurrence prototypes and for a memory of what fills which role (sections 5 and 7).

Speed, on the random 1% benchmark (1,831 messages; eval-results/2026-11-27-syntax-benchmark-random/summary.json): in the first judged run (the file as it stood before 8cd3377) the median time per message was 0.402 ms and opening the strategy took 51 ms; in the rerun of VSS at fa46cb7 (commit 8cd3377, the file's content at about 18:00) 0.392 ms and 58 ms. Both are single runs on a shared machine.

Grammar and NLP terms used below

Hidden Markov model (HMM)
A model of a sequence in which every word is produced by a hidden state, and each state depends only on the state before it. It has transition probabilities (state to state), emission probabilities (state to word) and start and end probabilities.
Expectation-maximization (EM), Baum-Welch
Training without labels: guess the parameters, compute for every word how likely each state is given the guess (the expectation step, done with the forward-backward algorithm), re-estimate the parameters from those expected counts (the maximization step), and repeat. Baum-Welch is EM for HMMs.
Forward-backward, posterior
An algorithm that gives, for every word, the probability of each state given the whole sentence. The code calls this probability of the chosen station its "confidence".
Viterbi decoding
Finding the single most probable state sequence of a sentence by dynamic programming.
Part-of-speech tag
The word class of a word in context: noun, verb, preposition, …
Chunking (shallow parsing)
Grouping words into non-overlapping phrases (noun groups, verb groups) without building a full tree.
Dependency parse
A tree in which every word depends on one other word (its head). The syntax schema is not a full dependency parse, but it uses the terms head, dependent, complement and modifier from dependency syntax.
Subcategorization frame
What a verb takes after it: an object, two objects ("give me a hand"), an object and a quality ("make it red"), a clause ("let it go").
Prepositional-phrase attachment
Deciding whether a phrase such as "on the table" belongs to the verb or to a noun before it.
Coordination, conjunct
Wires joined side by side; each joined wire is a conjunct.
Clause forms
imperative (a command), declarative (a statement), interrogative (a question), nonfinite (a verb form that cannot make a sentence alone: "facing left", "to delete it"), fragment (no verb).

5. Training, step by step

The numbers in this section come from a training run made for the first version of this document on the working tree (the output went to a scratch file, not to the repository). It took 79.3 s of wall time and used 1.6 GB of memory at its peak, on this machine while other work was running. The model it wrote was equal, key by key, to strategies/vss/model/model.json of the working tree at that moment. A reviewer repeated the run after fa46cb7 and got the same sentence and occurrence counts, the same log-likelihoods, and a model identical to the committed one.

Not obvious from the code: what training needs from the legend

Training depends on the legend (section 6) throughout. The terms used below, in short: a station is a hidden state of the HMM (a fine part-of-speech tag); a word list without open=true confines its words to the stations that list it, while an open list adds a station and confines nothing; a closed station admits only listed words (and words with a listed ending); a core station is one where a unit can end; like= makes a station share another's emission row; a form rule says where a word built with an ending (-ed, -ing, -er, -s) from another word of the text starts out, and confine=true restricts it to those stations.

Not obvious from the code

Training is deterministic. The "random" vectors of src/space.mjs are derived from the symbol's name by a fixed hash (FNV-1a) and a xorshift generator, and no step draws a random number. Retraining with the same code, legend and dataset gives the same model.

5.1 Messages

src/corpus.mjs readMessages reads every CSV file of semantic/drawing, semantic/diagram, semantic/document and semantic/calendar (or of the dataset root when it has no semantic/ folder), skips files whose first header is not text, and keeps each distinct text once. The trained model records 183,497 messages and 2,623,108 tokens (model.trained).

5.2 Spelling variants

train.mjs first observes every message, counts the words, and learns rough 128-dimensional word vectors (the method of section 5.7) for the words seen at least 5 times. src/variants.mjs findVariants then maps a rare word to a common one when:

  • the rare word is all lower-case letters, at least 5 letters long and seen at most 12 times;
  • the common word is all lower-case, at least 5 letters long and seen at least 100 times, and at least 40 times as often as the rare one;
  • the two are one edit apart (an insertion, a deletion, a substitution or a swap of two neighbouring letters);
  • the cosine of their vectors is at least 0.6 (0.15 is added when the legend fixes the place of the common word); a rare word without a vector is accepted only when both words have at least 6 letters and the legend lists the common word.

The model holds 114 variants, for example shoudl→should, bewteen→between, wihout→without. Some are wrong: foward→toward (it is "forward"), pleased→please, otters→others. From here on observe looks a variant up as its common word; the circuit still quotes the letters of the message.

5.3 Openers

Some messages begin with a few words and a break: "quick one: …", "from now on, …". train.mjs takes the words before the first comma, colon, semicolon, "!" or dash when they are 1 to 3 words, none of them a mark or a word the legend lists at a structural station (auxiliary, marker, infinitive, relative, negation, preposition, conjunction), and at least two tokens follow the break. Such a run is an opener when it was seen at least 5 times and followed by at least 4 different pairs of words. The model holds 134 openers: "from now on", "quick one", "actually", "also", "look", but also "circle", "260", "goal" and "roundup 260", which come from messages built on a common pattern in the dataset. At reading time only openers of 1 or 2 words are used (src/reader.mjs analyse, cut <= 2).

5.4 Vocabulary

src/routes.mjs buildVocabulary keeps every word seen at least 3 times (--min-count) and adds 16 classes for everything else: ~number ~date ~clock ~code ~mark ~quote ~capital ~upper ~initial ~word and the ending classes ~ing ~ed ~ly ~est ~er ~s. The model has 9,073 symbols. symbolOf looks a token up as itself; an unseen lower-case word as the first of its stems that is in the vocabulary and not confined by the legend; anything else as its class. A word written with a capital inside a sentence is a separate symbol from its lower-case form unless the legend lists the word (src/observe.mjs).

5.5 The station model: a hidden Markov model trained by expectation-maximization

The HMM reads one sentence at a time; src/legend.mjs sentencesOf ends a sentence after a run of terminator words ("." "!" "?"). The training corpus has 207,616 sentences. The states are the 25 stations of the legend (section 6). src/routes.mjs learnRoutes works as follows.

  1. Which stations a symbol may take. admitted in src/legend.mjs: a word in a word list without open=true may stand only at the stations that list it; a number, date, clock time, code, mark or quoted passage only at the stations that admit that shape; any other word at every station that is not closed, and at a closed station whose shape (capital, upper case) or suffix (-ly, -wise, …) admits it. In practice an ordinary unlisted lower-case word may be a describing word, a noun, a verb (opening its clause or after its subject), a predicative quality, or an adverb when its ending allows. A form rule with confine=true narrows further: a word built with -ed from another word of the text may stand only at the verb stations (predicate, following), as a describing word (modifier), or at complement (a predicative adjective), which shares the row of modifier.
  2. Shared rows. A station with like= shares the emission row of another: following (a verb after its subject) uses the words of predicate, and complement (a predicative quality) uses the words of modifier. Only the transitions tell them apart.
  3. The prior on transitions (prior): weight 4 to a station the source lists in follows, 1 from a station marked core to any other, 0.05 otherwise, and 0.05 to a station whose after list does not name the source. End: 1 for a core station, 0.05 otherwise. Start: the stations that may open a sentence.
  4. Initial emissions. Each symbol's count is shared among its admitted stations, with weight 8 (formLean) for a closed station that its suffix admits and for the station of a form rule (past and -ing forms lean to the verb station, -er/-est forms to modifier) when the word is built from another word of the text. A shape distribution (lower, capital, upper) per station starts at 0.15/0.65/0.20 for stations that admit capitals and 0.94/0.05/0.01 for the others.
  5. 25 rounds of Baum-Welch (forward-backward, scaled) over all sentences. In the maximization step the transitions are the expected counts plus 20 times the prior; a transition the prior marks as unlikely (0.05) is also damped by 0.05 in the counts, so the text cannot make it common. Three steps that are not part of EM change the expected emission counts: kinship 0.5 (half of the counts of a form such as "moved" are replaced by the distribution of its stem "move"), sharpening from round 7 on (the counts of every word that no list confines, open-list words such as "make" and "first" included, are raised to the power 1.5 and renormalised, so a word tends to keep one place; free in src/routes.mjs), and a small smoothing (0.001 times the initial emissions).

Log-likelihood per token in the run made for this document: −6.7092 after round 1, −5.3815 at rounds 15 and 16, −5.3823 after round 25.

Not obvious from the code

Because kinship and sharpening change the counts between the two EM steps, the likelihood is no longer guaranteed to rise: it falls slightly after round 16. The 25 rounds are a default (--iterations), not a convergence test.

An option --anchoring pulls each word towards the kind that the anchored frames show (section 5.9). It measured worse and is off unless the option is given (train.mjs comment; docs/strategies/vss.md).

Output in the model: transition (25 × 25), start, end, shape (25 × 3) and emission, one sparse row per symbol with pairs (station, probability) for the stations that own a row.

5.6 Occurrence prototypes

src/occurrence.mjs. Every message is decoded with the new HMM (readRoutes, without verb budget or bans). For each token whose posterior is at least 0.97, the outside of the occurrence is one 512-dimensional vector: the sum, for the neighbours at positions −2, −1, +1 and +2, of the side's vector bound to the neighbour's vector, plus the vector of the token's own form class (its ending or its capital), never the word itself. A neighbour counts as its word when the legend lists it, otherwise as its class. The prototype of a station is the mean of its occurrences minus the mean of all occurrences, normalised. 2,322,127 confident occurrences were used. The model stores the overall mean and the 25 prototypes as base64-encoded 32-bit floats (model.occurrence).

5.7 Word vectors

src/meaning.mjs learnMeanings. For every word seen at least 10 times that starts with a letter and is not confined by the legend: the vector is the sum, over its neighbours at −2…+2, of the positive pointwise mutual information of the pair (how much more often than chance the two stand together) times the side's vector bound to the neighbour's vector, in 128 dimensions, stored as 8-bit integers. The model has 5,244 word vectors. They are used to compare the heads of two nominals (section 7.8) and as the filler vectors of the memory.

5.9 Kinds from anchored frames

src/anchors.mjs anchoredFrames runs before the HMM is trained and counts, for every lower-case word that no list confines (open-list words included; free), how often it stood in a frame that the legend's words alone fix. "Can only be" below means that the legend confines the word to that station alone (only).

  • A thing: the one word between a word that can only be a determiner or only a degree word, and a word that closes the group (a mark, a word that can only be a preposition or a conjunction, a helping verb): "the ___ on".
  • A verb: after a word that can only be a helping verb (or a negation after a helping verb) before an object or a closing word; after "to" before an object; after a pronoun that can only be a subject ("they ___"), unless a preposition stands before that pronoun; message-initial or after a courtesy word before an object (weight 0.5). An object here is a word that can only be a determiner or degree word, a recipient pronoun, or the literal word "it".
  • A quality: after a word that can only be a degree word (with no condition on the word after); after a form of "be" before a closing word, when the word before the "be" is not a determiner (weight 0.5).

Words with at least 3 counts in total are kept in habits.kinds: 2,700 words, each with three counts [verb, thing, quality].

Not obvious from the code

The quality frame fires after only five words. The file's header says "after 'very' stands a quality", but the code does not count "very ___". "very", "really", "slightly" and "extremely" are only in an open list (@w.degree … open=true), so the legend does not confine them; "too", "quite", "more" and "so" are confined but also listed at adverb. The only words the legend confines to the degree station alone are the five compounds of @w.degree.compound: "a bit", "a lot", "a little bit", "a tad", "a touch". A reviewer checked this with anchoredFrames on test messages: "it is very zorbish ." gives [0, 0, 0], "it is too zorbish ." gives [0, 0, 0], and "make it a bit zorbish ." gives [0, 1, 1].

The last result shows a second effect: because opens also accepts words confined to the degree station, "a bit X ." counts X as a thing as well as a quality.

The second branch of the quality test, only(before, 'degree', 'adverb'), adds nothing: adverb is missing from the at map of src/anchors.mjs, so the test reduces to the first branch. A variable grades is defined and never used (void grades).

So the quality counts, which many rules read ("shown as a quality at least 3 times"), come almost only from "be ___ ." at half weight and from the five degree compounds. They are thin.

Why it is done this way

The file's header gives the reason: counting a word only in frames fixed by the legend "says what kind of word it is without asking the route model, so the model's own mistakes cannot confirm themselves". This holds for habits.kinds only; the habits of section 5.8, the anchored habit among them, are counted on the model's own readings. These counts, read through lexicon.kindsOf, are the evidence that most rules of the chunker and the linker consult ("seen as a verb at least 3 times"). kindsOf also lets the forms of a word share its verb count (the stem's count, and for a two-letter verb such as "go" its -es/-ing forms, fa46cb7) and a plural share its stem's thing count. Using the kinds as a prior inside EM measured worse, so they serve only the rules.

7. Reading, step by step

The public entry is strategies/vss/index.mjs: open() loads the model and creates the reader with {schema: 'syntax'}; analyse(text) returns {analyses: [{sop, confidence}]}, or no analysis and an error when the reader throws. The common reader lib/reader.mjs checks the circuit with validateSyntax. The steps below are in src/reader.mjs createVssReader → analyse.

7.1 Observation and tokens

  1. src/tokens.mjs tokenize uses the validator's pattern, so a token is exactly one word of lib/syntax.mjs wordsOf, except that punctuation is kept: a date, a clock time or a decimal number is one token, a clitic is split off ("don't" → "do", "n't"). shapeOf gives each token a shape: number, date, clock, code ("3px", "g1"), mark, quote (also addresses), upper, capital, initial (a capital at the start of a sentence), lower.
  2. src/observe.mjs joinWritten joins what is written as one word: "soft-focus", "Q1-Q4", "260,180", "example.com/prices".
  3. A quoted passage becomes one observation with the word ~quote; it can only be a literal.
  4. Compounds of the legend are joined, longest first: "next to", "in front of", "make sure".
  5. A spelling variant is looked up as its common word.
  6. A word written with a capital inside a sentence keeps its written form as its symbol, unless the legend lists its plain form; with rule L, it also keeps it when the legend lists the plain form only in open lists of stations that are not grammatical ("the title Second").

7.2 Openers and the occurrence lift

When the message begins with a learned opener of 1 or 2 words before the first break, those words are kept off the verb stations unless they carry the learned anchored habit (lexicon.anchoredVerb: read as a verb after a helping verb, a negation or "to" at least 2 times) or (fa46cb7) were seen as a verb in the anchored frames at least 20 times and never as a thing (SURELY_A_VERB, "excuse me, …"). Then, for every token and station, the cosine between the station's prototype and the token's outside vector (minus the overall mean) is computed; during decoding every emission probability is multiplied by exp(4 × cosine) (occurrenceWeight, default 4).

Not obvious from the code: two things called "anchored"

lexicon.anchoredVerb reads habits.anchored, which src/habits.mjs counts on the reader's own chunked readings of the text: a verb unit with a helping verb, a negation or "to" before it, at least 2 times. habits.kinds comes from src/anchors.mjs: frames fixed by legend words alone, counted on the raw text before the HMM exists. The opener rule uses the first; most rules of the chunker and the linker use the second (lexicon.kindsOf). The reason "the model's own mistakes cannot confirm themselves" (section 5.9) applies only to the second.

7.3 Decoding the stations

The verb budget is on by default (options.budget unset). budgetPath runs a second Viterbi over pairs (station, register), where the register counts the verbs of the current clause:

7.4 Cutting into units (chunking)

src/units.mjs buildUnits(tokens, stations, lexicon) walks the stations once:

  • a run of nominal stations becomes an entity (readEntity), with token positions for determiner, quantity, degree, modifier, compound, head, name, pronoun, literal, possessor;
  • auxiliaries, negations, adverbs before a verb and the verb become a verb unit (readVerbGroup), or an aux unit when no verb follows; a word that can be an auxiliary followed by another verb (or by a past form at complement) is the helping verb ("be turned", "have been moved");
  • a run of prepositions becomes a cue; grading words and qualities with no noun become a complement (or an adverb); interjections and terminators group; anything else is a one-token unit of its station's type.

Inside readEntity the last noun is the head, earlier nouns are compounds, names after the head form a name, a describing word is a compound or a modifier by the learned noun/adjective habit (a capitalised one is a compound), and several rules decide where one nominal ends and the next begins (section 9.3). After the walk, 13 merge passes join units ("Jan's office chair", "two more clouds", "tomorrow morning") and about 35 checks raise doubts (sections 9.3 and 9.4).

7.6 Linking units into clauses, nominals, relations and coordinations

src/links.mjs linkUnits(text, tokens, units, context, flips) produces internal wires nlu.clause, nlu.entity, nlu.relation and nlu.coordination. Every quoted value keeps the positions of the tokens it covers ([key, quoted, first, last]), so that emit-syntax.mjs can map it to words later. It works in this order:

  1. Preparations over all units: a quality right after a preposition is a nominal ("in red"); a bare word listed after a nominal with a conjunction is a nominal ("by product and region"); a postposed word joins the pronoun before it ("everything else", fa46cb7).
  2. Sentences are split at terminator units; each is linked by linkSentence, then tieLoose ties loose clauses.
  3. Helping verbs: an auxiliary before its subject joins the verb after the subject (the clause is inverted: a question); an auxiliary with no verb becomes the verb.
  4. Clause roles: for each verb unit, its role (main, describing a nominal, infinitive, completing another verb) and where its clause starts. A main clause's subject and the words before its verb are found by scanLeft.
  5. Clause wires: material before the first verb (fronted phrases, or a fragment clause), the clause form (section 7.7), markers, then fill(clause, region) for the units from after the verb up to the start of the next clause.
  6. How the clauses hang together: describing clauses, purpose, completing clauses, relative clauses, fronted clauses, clauses joined by a linking word, coordinated clauses.
  7. Separators left over are placed on the nearest standing clause.

fill walks the region left to right with a small state: the objects of the clause, the frontier of nominals seen so far, the last nominal, a pending preposition, pending joiners, the last relation. A nominal becomes a role of the clause, the end of a pending relation, a conjunct of a coordination, or a value tied to the nominal before it. A joiner (conjunction or comma) is first tested for gapping (section 9.7) and for joining clauses. A preposition waits for its nominal and then attach decides where the relation starts (section 7.9).

Not obvious from the code

The linker is one left-to-right pass of fixed policy. It never revisits an earlier choice except by raising a doubt, which makes the decoder run again and the whole linking start over.

The internal wires keep the nlu slot names because the linker was written for that schema. The nlu text output (src/emit.mjs) still exists: tests/vss.test.mjs, evaluate.mjs, judged.mjs and triage.mjs read it, and in that mode the reader also returns a second reading (the least certain decision taken the other way, when its margin is below 0.7) and flags. In the syntax mode used by the strategy, flips is always empty and only one circuit is written.

7.8 Coordination: which earlier nominal a listed one joins

When a nominal follows a conjunction or a comma, fill has to decide which earlier nominal it is listed with. The candidates are the frontier: with a comma alone, only the nominals of the clause that are not ends of relations; otherwise every nominal seen in the clause, except the end of a set-off place phrase (fa46cb7: a phrase set off by commas whose preposition points at a known thing, a pronoun or a nominal with a definite determiner, before a new thing: ", next to the crate, a small black cat"). partner chooses:

  1. a bare name joins the capitalised name right before it ("items Plan and Review");
  2. otherwise each candidate gets a likeness score, context.alike: the cosine of the two head words' vectors (128-d) plus the cosine of the two nominals' build vectors, plus 0.02 × its position among the candidates divided by the number of nominals in the frontier (at most 0.02 in all), so that the nearer of two equal candidates wins; on an exact tie the later one wins as well. A build vector is the bundle, in 512 dimensions, of each slot bound to what fills it: the determiner word, "some" for a quantity, the index of each modifier and compound, and the kind of core (head, name, pronoun, literal). Two nominals built alike ("a large yellow cat", "a small brown dog") have near build vectors;
  3. if the nearest candidate (or the list it is in) is introduced like the new nominal (same determiner, or both bare, both names, both pronouns, …) and the most alike one is not, the nearest wins ("with title Plan and participants Ana");
  4. a determiner-led nominal that is unlike every candidate (best score below 0.3) is not joined to the end of a phrase; it joins the last candidate that is not one ("at 10:00 and the room to Cedar room");
  5. otherwise the most alike candidate.

The same likeness decides an aside: after the verb's object, a bare nominal set off by a comma that is unlike every candidate (below 0.3) is not one more thing the verb acts on; it is tied to the clause with no linking word. 7.9 Where a relation starts

attach(relation, clause, entity, cue, target) in src/links.mjs, in order:

  1. the second linking word of a pair starts where the first did ("from largest to smallest");
  2. no nominal before the preposition, or a pronoun: the clause ("move it to the left");
  3. "of" (mark belongs): the nominal before it ("the level of Intro");
  4. in a fragment: the nominal before, or a nominal that one is itself placed by (up to 4 steps back, 0.3 subtracted per step), whichever holds this preposition best;
  5. two literals, that is numbers, dates, clock times or quoted text, one against the other: the first literal ("240 by 160");
  6. otherwise every host competes: the clause, with the verb's score, and each nominal of the chain (the nominal before, the one it is placed by, …, up to 4), with the noun's score. The score is holds: the counted attachment rate of the word with this preposition (log, smoothed) plus 2 × the memory's fit of the target's vector to that role. If the two best scores differ by less than 0.5, the relation starts at the clause and is marked attachment=open, written as attachment=ambiguous.

On the random benchmark, 67 of VSS's 1,206 accepted circuits (5.6%) carried attachment=ambiguous in the first judged run; after the rerun at fa46cb7, 66 of 1,202 (5.5%) do, with 225 circuits still waiting for a judge (eval-results/2026-11-27-syntax-benchmark-random/table.md).

Why it is done this way

The project's standard (docs/strategies/vss.md, "What the reader leaves open"; Rule 2 of docs/syntax-schema.md): a reader that sees one message states what the words settle and marks the rest, so that a later layer with context can complete the circuit without undoing a guess. A relation marked ambiguous still belongs to its clause.

7.10 Writing the syntax circuit

src/emit-syntax.mjs emitSyntax(text, tokens, wires, knows) maps every token span to words of wordsOf(text) (punctuation is not a word, so a span that covers only punctuation disappears), translates each internal wire (section 8), and then runs a series of passes over the syntax wires (section 9.10). knows gives it seven lookups from the reader: whether a question word stands for a thing, whether a word is a verb form, a quality, a naming participle, a conjunction or a question word, and whether a verb takes a result. Every word still uncovered becomes a discourse word of the clause it stands in. writeCircuit (lib/syntax.mjs) orders the wires so that the schema's alignment rule (a quoted value claims the earliest free occurrence of its words) gives each value its own occurrence. It throws when a span lies outside the message, when a word is uncovered or claimed twice. When repeated words cross between wires, so that no order gives every value its own occurrence, it writes the wires in message order (since commit d12c190) and throws only if the check of that text then finds words that are uncovered or not in the message. When it throws, the strategy returns no circuit and the error.

Not obvious from the code: what the numbers can and cannot be compared with

The round reports are not comparable with the benchmark. In a round report a circuit settled by agreement of several strategy families counts as accepted for the strategies that wrote it, and VSS is one of those families; with --focus vss the judge always reads VSS's circuits so that VSS gets a level (tests/benchmark/syntax/round.mjs). The round files were also small, short-message files. improvements-ideas.md notes that the dataset as a whole is dominated by long drawing and chart messages, where VSS does worse. The part about length is not borne out on the random sample: its drawing and diagram messages average 13.9 and 12.4 words against 13.1 for all, while VSS's GOAL is 68.2% on drawing and 60.9% on diagram messages (syntax-work-2026-11-27.html, I3). Acceptance by message length was not measured.

A judged rerun on this sample is not an independent measure of the later rules, for two reasons. The rules committed after the first run were checked against its judged circuits (dd562c3), and some were found in its rejections (the fa46cb7 message: "rules found in the benchmark rejections"). And the judges' rules themselves changed after the first run was judged (commit a81dd93: "let's add", "everything else", embedded question clauses), so a rerun is judged by different rules. improvements-ideas.md proposes a fresh sample.

Measurements of the nlu period (not comparable)

From docs/strategies/vss.md, measured on the earlier nlu schema: blind paired reviews of 240 messages, every circuit acceptable for 45.0% (first model) and 50.8% (model at d98b426) against 67.5% and 63.8% for spaCy direct; the judged loop on the fixed sample, 52.9% under strict rules (model 03f9abe) and 60.0% under the plausible rules with open marks (model 346876e); against saved circuits, 64.6% F1 of links with their role. An independent review then estimated a ceiling of 60 to 65% for this design (one committed station path, a single-pass linker, habits counted on the reader's own readings), 70 to 78% for a design that keeps a lattice of stations and assembles circuits from constructions declared in the legend, and 83 to 88% "if the factors of that design were trained on the drafted circuits" (drafted circuits: proposals saved in the nlu period, not accepted ones). That review found "that most lost acceptance comes from the station of a word, not from linking". Both its ceiling and that finding were set in the nlu schema; VSS's 65.9% in the syntax schema is already above the 60–65% ceiling, and the lever study of section 14 places most of the faults in the syntax schema in units and links instead.

14. Known limits

  1. One path, one pass. Each round decodes one station path; the linker is one pass of fixed policy; alternative readings are never built and compared. The independent review in docs/strategies/vss.md put the ceiling of this design at 60 to 65%, but in the nlu schema; in the syntax schema VSS already scored 65.9% in the first judged run.
  2. Where the faults are (measured). The lever study (eval-results/2026-11-27-vss-levers-study/study-result.json, commit 45677bc; summary in improvements-ideas.md, "Done since…") forced VSS's stations to those of a known accepted circuit on the 538 benchmark messages where VSS was not accepted but such a circuit exists. For 292 of them (54%) the circuit did not change at all: the stations already fit and the error is in src/units.mjs or src/links.mjs. Perfect stations turned 50 messages into accepted ones with certainty: +2.7 points (65.9% to 68.6%), and 191 forced circuits were new. Correction, after judging: the forced-station circuits were then judged in full (eval-results/2026-11-27-vss-lever-trials, strategy vss-oracle-stations, commit 9df0889): of 401 such circuits, 170 were accepted, so perfect stations would repair up to about 170 messages, roughly +9 points, not +2.7. Both parts matter: for about half of the failures the stations already fit and the fault is in src/units.mjs or src/links.mjs; for the other half better tagging is a real lever (the same trials measured DistilBERT evidence on the roles of words at +3.9 points). The faults left form "a long tail: 17 rule groups of 5 to 32 messages each (of about 520 rejected)".
  3. Self-counted habits. Habits and the memory are counted on the reader's own readings, with a plainer chunker than the one that reads (section 5.8). The learned lists carry noise ("drawing" as a clause-taking verb, "nice" as a verb that asks, "igve" as a giving verb).
  4. Thresholds set by hand. Three are documented as chosen from counts in this dataset: SURELY_A_THING = 20 (src/reader.mjs), SURELY_A_VERB = 20 (src/links.mjs, fa46cb7) and UNLIKE = 0.3 (src/links.mjs). Most rules use counts such as "at least 3" whose origin the code does not record. Training has its own set (prior weights, 20 as pseudo-count weight, sharpening, kinship, the variant and opener thresholds).
  5. Rules as code. The 170 rows of section 9 (some rows group several checks) are JavaScript, ordered, many reading the same structures. Their interactions show only by running (section 7.8 shows a rule whose own example is caught first by another). Only eight groups carry letters that can be switched off.
  6. English in the code. Ending classes, clitics, stem spelling, the plural test, the question mark, the word "it" in the anchored frames and the break marks of openers are in JavaScript, outside the legend (section 10).
  7. The legend is not independent of the code. The code hard-codes the legend's station names, mark names and the nlu slot names (box at the end of section 6), so the legend cannot be edited or replaced apart from the code, and the src/legend.mjs header ("no word list and no slot name of its own") holds for word lists only.
  8. The model carries the legend. An edit to legend.sop needs retraining before it changes any reading.
  9. Dataset-shaped learned items. Some openers ("circle", "260", "roundup 260") and spelling variants (foward→toward, otters→others) come from the dataset's patterns or are wrong; misspellings under 5 letters are never corrected.
  10. Observed faults (checked for the first version of this document): "the two lines differ" → head="differ"; "make runway orange" → one modifier run "runway orange"; "make sure it fits" → one nominal used as complement and as subject; "pie radius smaller" → "smaller" as a dependent of the nominal, while the schema document's own example writes it as a modifier of the fragment clause (docs/syntax-schema.md, the syntax.clause section: @c1 syntax.clause form=fragment complement=$n1 modifier="smaller"; Rule 5.2 states the principle with "make the box red").
  11. The whole dataset against small files. 65.9% on the random sample against 84–87% on the small files of the first two rounds (improvements-ideas.md). improvements-ideas.md puts this down to long messages, but on the random sample the drawing and diagram messages are not longer than the others; VSS is weakest on diagram messages (60.9%; syntax-work-2026-11-27.html, I3). The effect of length itself was not measured.
  12. The benchmark figure leans on agreement labels. 586 of VSS's 1,206 accepted circuits in the first run were not read by a judge in that run: they equal labels the messages had before the run, and 567 of those 593 labels VSS had written in agreement with spacy-direct (section 12).
  13. Portability. The reader is meant to run without file access, but src/emit-syntax.mjs → lib/syntax.mjs reads the schema file with node:fs at load time (section 1). Passing the schema text in, or bundling it, would restore the property; not done.
  14. Leftovers. asideW is a constant false in fill; budget.known has no effect by default; anchors.mjs keeps an unused grades and a second quality branch that adds nothing (section 5.9); the pair between … and never fires (section 6.4); src/units.mjs.orig, src/syntax.mjs and judging-rules-syntax.md belong to earlier states.

15. Steps towards a reusable engine

improvements-ideas.md ("Making VSS reusable", written after the owner asked whether the rules are general or particular) states that the rules added so far are statements about English that name only function words and use what VSS learns from text, and that what is not yet general is: some thresholds measured on this dataset, the choice of which constructions to cover (driven by the frequent faults of commands to a drawing application), and a few borderline legend words (left/right as direction adverbs, today/tomorrow, thanks). It proposes four steps:

  1. Audit of generality. List every rule and word list and try to show each one particular; class each as general English, a threshold tuned on this data, or particular; rewrite or remove the particular ones. Section 9 of this document is a starting inventory; its marks separate the documented thresholds from the undocumented ones.
  2. Rules as data. Move the rules out of JavaScript into constructions declared in the legend (SOP Lang), over stations and word marks, so that the engine stays generic and the grammar of English becomes a file that can be read, checked and extended. The word marks of section 6.2 already move part of the knowledge this way. A precondition: the code now hard-codes the legend's station, mark and slot names (box at the end of section 6); constructions declared as data would have to name those through the legend, or the legend could still not change apart from the code.
  3. Thresholds learned, not set. The code names the features of each decision; the weights are learned from accepted circuits, so that no number is tuned by hand on this dataset.
  4. A test outside the dataset. Run VSS on English it was never tuned on (other kinds of messages or a public corpus) and have a judge read a sample.

The same file lists ideas not yet tried, and the lever study (section 14) estimated the gain of four of them on the benchmark, starting from 65.9%:

The trial circuits of these levers are being judged (eval-results/2026-11-27-vss-lever-trials) to decide which to build; the plan recorded in improvements-ideas.md is "first the measured rule prototypes, one commit each; then the structural rules; then learned decisions where thresholds fail; station-level learning only if the trials justify it". Two related trials were measured and dropped: counting word kinds also from the accepted circuits changed 41 of 1,831 benchmark circuits "with no net gain", and relating "goes" to "go" in the stem rule shifted unrelated readings.

Any training on the labels meets a circularity: 55,648 of the 62,066 labels came from agreement of VSS and spacy-direct (and 880 more from VSS and an older DistilBERT), so a VSS supervised by them would partly learn its own output, and its later agreement with spaCy would mean less. The nlu-period review quoted in section 12 pointed to a lattice of stations with whole circuits assembled from constructions declared in the legend; its estimates were made in the nlu schema, before the lever study placed most faults in units and links.

Also needed for a fair measure, from this document: the English knowledge now in JavaScript (section 10) would move into the legend or a language file, and the benchmark sample used to check rules would be replaced by a fresh held-out one.
